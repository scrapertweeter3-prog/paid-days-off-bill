Paid days off are an $18,510 line in the hiring bill

A senior engineer's fully loaded cost gets sliced against subscription pricing all the time, but one slice rarely gets its own column: the days the seat is paid for while nobody sits in it. The salary behind an $18,290 month is $219,480 a year. Spread across the 260 weekdays a calendar hands out, $219,480 / 260 = $844.15, so every paid weekday the desk sits empty carries an $844.15 price tag on its own.

The desk itself works fewer days than the calendar offers. Eleven federal holidays come off the top, which leaves 249 working days, and a typical senior package adds fifteen PTO days and six sick ones. $219,480 / 249 = $881.45 for each day the work can actually happen. The 21 absent days still draw full pay: 21 x $881.45 = $18,510 of salary attached to days with no output, and the benefits stack on top of that total whether anyone shows up or not.

The comparison model has no such days. It runs the same calendar, Monday through Friday with US national holidays off, and it has no PTO line because nobody on it is an employee. Count only the days a person is actually producing and the hire's year is $219,480 across 228 days at a desk, $962.63 a day. The same year of the subscription on monthly billing, $71,940, is $315.53 across those identical 228 days. The hire pays three times as much for a productive day, before a single meeting is counted.

Those absent days price out at $18,510 a year, and $18,510 / $5,995 = 3.1 months of the monthly plan. The plan behind that math is one active request at a time on a Trello board, code delivered through a private GitHub repo, turnaround promised in 72 hours, revisions until sign-off, and cancel anytime. Billing follows requests rather than seats, so there is no line item at all for a day nobody worked.

The time-off arithmetic belongs to Poket Dev, an unlimited software development subscription that bills for output instead of for days on a calendar.
